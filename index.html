The History of Pizza

From ancient flatbreads to a global culinary icon

1. Ancient Origins

Although modern pizza is deeply tied to Italy, the practice of baking flatbreads with toppings dates back thousands of years across the Mediterranean:

  • Ancient Egypt: Flatbreads shaped and colored like the sun were prepared after the discovery of yeast.
  • Ancient Greece: Citizens ate plakuntos, a dough garnished with herbs, spices, garlic, and onions.
  • Persian Empire: Soldiers under King Darius I the Great cooked flatbreads on their shields, topping them with melted cheese and dates.

2. The Birthplace: Naples

Pizza as we know it today was born in the city of Naples, Italy, during the 18th century. In its beginnings, it was a humble, affordable food eaten by working-class street vendors and laborers.

The game changer was the introduction of the tomato from the Americas to Europe. Initially, people believed tomatoes were poisonous and only used them as decorative plants. However, necessity and resourcefulness led Neapolitan peasants to add them to their flatbreads, creating the foundation of modern pizza.

The Origin of Pizza Margherita (1889)

In June 1889, Neapolitan pizza chef Raffaele Esposito was asked to prepare pizzas for the visiting King Umberto I and Queen Margherita of Savoy. The queen chose her favorite: a combination that beautifully represented the colors of the Italian flag.

  • 🔴 Tomato: Red
  • ⚪ Mozzarella: White
  • 🟢 Basil: Green

In her honor, this iconic style was named Pizza Margherita.

3. Global Domination

1905

Mass Italian immigration brought the recipe to the United States. In this year, Lombardi's opened in Little Italy (New York City), officially recognized as the first pizzeria in North America.

Post-World War II

Allied soldiers returning home from Europe brought back a massive craving for pizza, popularizing it across Western countries and sparking the rise of global fast-food franchises.

Today

Today, pizza stands as one of the most popular and adapted dishes on Earth, evolving alongside local cultures with endless variations of crusts and creative toppings.
